# The Stages of NLP: narration

## intro
> Warm and welcoming.
[c01] Welcome to NLP, Simply Explained. This is unit {{episode.unit}}, lesson {{episode.lesson}}: the stages of NLP.

[c02] When you read a sentence, you understand it in a blink. A computer can't. It has to work its way up, one stage at a time.

## idea
[c03] Here is the one idea to remember from this lesson. Understanding a sentence is not one step. It is a staircase.

## stages
[c04] Let's climb it. There are five steps, and each one builds on the step below.

[c05] Step one is lexical analysis. The computer splits the text into words and tokens, and works out where sentences begin and end.

[c06] Step two is morphological analysis. It looks inside each word for its parts. "Barking" is "bark" plus "i n g".

[c07] Step three is syntactic analysis. It works out the structure of the sentence: who is doing what, and which words belong together.

[c08] Step four is semantic analysis. It works out the literal meaning of the words and the sentence.

[c09] And step five is pragmatic analysis: what the speaker actually means in this situation, which can go beyond the literal words.

## walk
[c10] Let's send one sentence up the staircase: "The dogs were barking loudly". The table shows what each stage finds.

[c11] The lexical stage finds five words: the, dogs, were, barking, loudly.

[c12] The morphological stage notices that "dogs" is "dog" plus a plural "s", and "barking" is "bark" plus "i n g".

[c13] The syntactic stage works out that "dogs" is the subject: they are the ones doing the barking.

[c14] The semantic stage understands the meaning: some animals were making a loud noise.

[c15] And the pragmatic stage reads between the lines. If someone says this to you at home, they might mean: go and check who's at the door.

## ambiguity
> Slow down for the example.
[c16] So why is this hard for a computer? One word explains most of it: ambiguity. Ambiguity is when the same words can be read in more than one way.

[c17] Take this sentence: "I saw her duck."

[c18] It's like an optical illusion. One picture, two images, and you can't see both at once.

## duck
[c19] Here's how that plays out in a conversation. You type: "I saw her duck." A careful assistant has to ask: do you mean her pet duck, or that she ducked down?

## levels
[c20] Ambiguity hides at different stages. Here are the two you'll meet most often.

[c21] Lexical ambiguity is one word with two meanings, like "bank": the side of a river, or the place that keeps your money.

[c22] Syntactic ambiguity is one sentence with two structures, like "I saw the man with the telescope". Who has the telescope? {@verdict}Each stage clears up its own kind of ambiguity.

## quiz
[c23] Time for a quick quiz. Which stage decides that "bank" means the side of a river?

[c24 after=3] Pause the video and pick one. Lexical, syntactic, semantic, or pragmatic?

[c25] The answer is semantic. Choosing the right meaning of a word is semantic analysis.

## recap
[c26] Let's recap. NLP understands text in layers, like a staircase.

[c27] The five steps are lexical, morphological, syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic.

[c28] Each stage clears up its own kind of ambiguity.

[c29] And a mistake on a low step travels up to every step above it.
